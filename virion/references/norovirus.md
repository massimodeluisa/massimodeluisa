# Norovirus

The second figure. A naked icosahedral capsid, not an influenza envelope. HHMI is not a source for this one.

## What is drawn

One Norwalk capsid and one membrane patch. Dock on histo-blood group antigen, endocytosis into a pit, the shell opens and one RNA strand leaves, a new capsid leaves through a gap when the cell lyses.

## Measured

- ViralZone norovirus page: 38–40 nm, T=3, 180 VP1, genome 7.3–8.3 kb, positive-sense RNA, histo-blood group antigens, endocytosis that is not clathrin- or caveolin-dependent, release by lysis. A smaller empty particle is about 23 nm, T=1, 60 VP1. That small particle is not drawn.
- Prasad et al., J Virol 1994: empty Norwalk capsid, 38.0 nm diameter, 90 dimers, T=3, shell density from 11.0 to 15.0 nm radius, arch-like capsomeres at the twofold axes.
- PDB 1IHM, Prasad et al., Science 1999: crystal structure of that capsid, 3.4 Å, chains A, B, and C.

The drawn shell radius is 15 nm. The arches reach 19 nm, which is the 38 nm outer diameter. The facing arches are a sample of the 90 dimers.

## Left out

Replication factories, the polyprotein, VPg, and the 23 nm empty shell. The lysis stage is a new particle. The figure does not show how that particle was built.

## Not a source of sequence

UniProt lists VP1 and PDB ids. The facts module keeps the accession path as a URL only. It does not contain the chain.
